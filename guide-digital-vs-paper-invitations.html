Digital vs paper wedding invitations: an honest comparison

You do not have to choose one. Many couples now send a paper invitation to close family and a digital link to everyone, with the same QR code on both.

Side by side

Digital (link)Paper
CostOne-time, lowPrinting and postage per guest
SpeedArrives instantlyDays or weeks by post
RSVPsOne tap, arrives in your phoneReply cards, often late
ChangesSend a new linkReprint
Guests abroadCountdown in venue time, map buttonSlow international post
KeepsakeScreenshotSomething to frame

When digital is the better choice

Short notice, many guests abroad, a destination wedding, or a tight budget. Also when you need practical details guests will check again: schedule, map, dress code, hotels.

When paper still wins

Grandparents, very formal weddings, and anyone who will keep the invitation. Paper feels like a gift.

The best of both

Print a simple paper invitation with a QR code. The paper carries the feeling; the link carries the details and the RSVP. You update the link if plans change, and the paper never goes out of date.

Questions

Is it rude to send digital wedding invitations?

No. It is now common, especially for guests abroad. For close elders, pair it with a paper card or a phone call.

How early should I send a digital invitation?

Six to eight weeks before the wedding, or ten to twelve weeks for a destination wedding, with a save the date earlier.

Can I add a QR code to paper invitations?

Yes. Download the QR code from the maker and place it on your paper invitation or save the date.
